2) Haze, heat, and air quality become the main domestic irritant
This is probably the most important “real-life” story for ordinary Singaporeans next week. The headlines already point to a serious transboundary haze problem linked to Indonesian peat fires, El Niño, and poor water conditions in Borneo.

What I expect:

Air quality will remain elevated at times, with periods of unhealthy or very unhealthy PSI.
If winds shift favorably or rain comes from the west/northwest, there may be brief relief.
If winds stay unfavorable and fires persist in Sumatra/Kalimantan, conditions could worsen into the race weekend.
Schools and universities will likely issue more indoor-activity guidance.
Outdoor events, corporate activities, and even some public gatherings may be adjusted.
Mask sales, air purifier demand, and N95 usage will rise again.
Social media will get noisy, with complaints about the skyline, visibility, and health impacts.
The government will likely issue frequent updates and may remind the public of haze-response measures.
What this means politically:
This is not a new problem for Singapore, but it is politically sensitive because people remember 2013 and 2015. If the haze gets bad enough to affect F1 entertainment or school routines, public frustration will rise quickly.

My best read:
Singapore will not be in “total shutdown” mode next week, but it will be in heightened alert / managed annoyance mode. The most likely scenario is intermittent deterioration, with at least one or two bad days around the F1 weekend.

Confidence:

Air quality remains a major issue: very high
Some operational adjustments to schools/events: high
Bad enough to force notable restrictions: medium-high
Full-scale severe haze emergency response: medium-low, unless Indonesian fires flare badly or winds turn against Singapore
