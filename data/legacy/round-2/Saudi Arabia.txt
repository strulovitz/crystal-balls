My overall prediction for Saudi Arabia over the next 7 days is this:

Saudi Arabia will enter a phase of heightened but controlled military confrontation with the Houthis. In short: more fighting on the Yemen front, more claimed strikes inside Saudi Arabia, more diplomatic signaling, but no sudden collapse of oil exports or full-blown regional war in the next week.

Here is what I expect to happen, ranked by likelihood.

1. The Saudi-led coalition will keep striking Houthi targets heavily
Likelihood: very high

I expect Saudi Arabia and its allies to continue air operations against Houthi positions in Yemen, especially around:

Bab el-Mandeb / Mokha
Taiz
Hodeidah area
Sanaa if the Houthis keep firing at Riyadh or other major Saudi targets
The headlines already show that Saudi Arabia has sent fighter jet support to the Yemeni government offensive and is actively intercepting missiles. That suggests the next week will not be a pause. It will be an escalation cycle.

What this probably looks like:

Daily or near-daily coalition statements about intercepted missiles, drones, and strikes on Houthi launch sites
More claims that Houthi armor, air defenses, warehouses, or command nodes were hit
Saudi Arabia supporting the internationally recognized Yemeni government’s push around Mokha and Taiz
The Houthis claiming to have hit back at Saudi targets even as they lose ground in some areas
Saudi Arabia will likely frame this as “supporting the Yemeni government” rather than “fighting the Houthis directly,” but on the ground it will be much more involved.

2. The Houthis will keep attacking Saudi Arabia, especially airports and oil-related sites
Likelihood: very high

This is probably the biggest near-term story inside Saudi Arabia itself.

I expect at least several more Houthi missile or drone attacks in the next week on:

Riyadh airport
Jazan
Najran
Abha or other southern airports
Aramco facilities, refineries, or oil infrastructure near Riyadh / western Saudi Arabia
Possibly Jeddah if the Houthis want to widen the pressure
The pattern in the headlines is already clear: the Houthis are not just claiming attacks on the border. They are claiming strikes deep inside the kingdom, including Riyadh and Aramco-linked sites. That means the next week will likely bring more of the same.

What I expect specifically:

At least one more claimed Houthi strike on a Saudi airport
At least one more claimed attack on an oil or energy site
Some real damage, but probably not enough to shut major operations for long
Saudi officials saying most projectiles were intercepted while still acknowledging injuries or localized damage
More public debate inside Saudi Arabia about air defense readiness
The key question is not whether the Houthis will fire. They almost certainly will.
The question is whether they land a meaningful hit on Aramco infrastructure.

My base case: yes, there will be more attacks; probably some minor to moderate damage; but no catastrophic shutdown of Saudi oil production in the next 7 days.

3. Turkey and Pakistan will move from announcement to visible deployment
Likelihood: high

This is one of the most important geopolitical developments in the headlines.

I expect Turkey and Pakistan to begin deploying forces, or at least rapid reaction units, command staff, air defense assets, and liaison teams into Saudi Arabia under the Mecca pact.

However, I would not expect a large-scale ground army entering Yemen immediately in the next week. The Financial Times headline already hints that Turkey has not fully ratified the deal, which makes a big ground deployment less likely right away.

So my prediction is:

Turkey and Pakistan announce deployments
Small to medium units arrive or begin positioning
Saudi Arabia hosts them visibly enough to send a signal
Their role is mostly defensive, advisory, command-and-control, and deterrence-related at first
Pakistan may be more willing to commit troops than Turkey
Turkey may contribute aircraft, air defense systems, intelligence, and senior military personnel
This will matter a lot for Saudi public messaging. Riyadh can now say it is not alone. That reduces pressure on the kingdom to do everything by itself.

4. Saudi Arabia will keep its oil export strategy focused on the East-West pipeline
Likelihood: very high

The headlines make this very clear: Saudi Arabia is increasingly leaning on its East-West pipeline to bypass Red Sea / Bab el-Mandeb disruption.

I expect in the next week:

Continued high flows through the East-West pipeline
More official statements emphasizing that exports are “normal” or “recovering”
Saudi Arabia using lower Asian prices to keep customers buying Saudi crude even while shipping risks remain elevated
OPEC+ keeping November output targets steady for now
Oil markets staying nervous but not fully panic-mode unless there is a major Aramco hit
This is probably the most important economic story.

Saudi Arabia’s next-week oil posture will likely be:

Defend market share in Asia with lower prices
Use the East-West pipeline as the main export relief valve
Avoid an aggressive OPEC+ response unless damage is serious
Signal to customers that supply is resilient
If a major refinery or export terminal is hit badly, then yes, Riyadh could move faster on production or pricing. But in the base case, I expect continuity.

Higher risk premiums if Houthi strikes continue

Pressure from G7 stock releases, especially diesel
Saudi Asian price cuts acting as a ceiling on how much prices can run up
So the market will be pulled in two directions:

War risk pushing prices up
Rising exports and Saudi discounts pushing prices down
My base case for the next 7 days:

No sustained oil price spike unless there is a confirmed major hit to Aramco infrastructure or a sudden Hormuz escalation
More volatility than calm
Diesel and product markets possibly more stressed than crude because of shipping disruption and G7 release dynamics
In plain terms: oil stays nervous, but Saudi Arabia’s export workaround keeps the market from fully panicking.

The FlyDubai cockpit attack landing in Saudi Arabia is a big deal for domestic security messaging, even if the main investigation is UAE/Israel-related.

Continued Saudi investigation of the co-pilot / suspect
Closer coordination with UAE and possibly India / Oman authorities
Tighter airport security procedures, especially at major hubs like Riyadh, Jeddah, and Tabuk
More attention to cockpit access protocols and crew vetting
Possible temporary rerouting or extra caution around southern airports if Houthi attacks continue
This incident will feed into a broader Saudi narrative:

“Our airspace and airports are under pressure”
“Security is being strengthened”
“The kingdom can handle both external attack risk and international aviation incidents”
It may also make Saudi Arabia more sensitive to any new cockpit or hijacking scare in the region.

Likelihood: high

Riyadh’s messaging in the next week will probably balance two goals:

Show strength against the Houthis

Say it “will not hesitate” to respond firmly

Avoid a single dramatic act that forces Iran into direct war immediately

Looks strong domestically
Does not require heavy US ground or air commitment
Keeps oil moving through alternative routes
