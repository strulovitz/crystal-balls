Okay, reading all of it like a war-room forecast: next week for Russia is going to be tense, election-driven, and very much about “holding the line” while the pressure from Ukraine, NATO, the US, and the energy market builds.

I’m treating “this country” as Russia.

Short answer:
In the next 7 days, Russia will most likely hold its State Duma elections, spin them as a wartime mandate for Putin, continue heavy drone/missile pressure on Ukraine, test NATO nerves near Poland/Lithuania, try to sell a fragile “energy truce,” face a US House vote on sanctions, and feel more domestic economic stress from fuel prices, refinery strikes, and mobilization rumors.

No full collapse. No instant all-out NATO war. But a very bumpy week.

Most likely next-week sequence for Russia
1) September 18–20: Duma elections become the main domestic event
This is the biggest scheduled event in Russia next week.

What I expect:

United Russia wins a comfortable, probably supermajority-like result.
Turnout will be pushed hard by local administrations, workplaces, and state media.
The Kremlin will frame the result as “the country supports Putin’s war course.”
Opposition parties will remain weak, fragmented, or regionally limited.
Communists, LDPR, and the New People / pro-Kremlin list will still get symbolic seats.
Blunt read:
This election is not really about choice. It is about legitimacy, regional control, and giving Putin a renewed parliamentary mandate while the war continues.

What could happen on the ground:

Small protests in Moscow, St Petersburg, maybe a few regions where drone strikes or economic pain hit harder.
More online anger than street anger.
Security services will be alert but probably not over-the-top unless there is a visible incident.
Drone attacks near Russian cities during election week could create local frustration, especially if fuel depots, logistics centers, or shopping areas are hit.
Likelihood:

High: United Russia retains control of the Duma.
High: Kremlin uses results to claim a war mandate.
Medium: small but noticeable protests or online backlash.
Low: major spontaneous nationwide protest wave in just one week.
2) The “energy truce” will be attempted, but it will be fragile
The headlines show Trump claiming Ukraine and Russia agreed not to hit energy targets, while Kyiv says it is more like a proposal for mutual restraint. That gap matters.

What I expect next week:

A short-lived pause or reduction in strikes on fuel depots, refineries, gas infrastructure, and power plants will be tried.
The US will push hard because diesel prices and global energy markets are getting nervous.
Russia will probably accept a limited pause publicly, but keep enough flexibility to strike if it feels Ukraine is not complying.
Ukraine will likely ease up on Russian refineries for a few days, especially under Trump pressure, but may continue hitting other targets: air defense, rail, command nodes, logistics, maybe fuel storage in different form.
Most likely outcome:

The truce looks real for 24–72 hours.
Then both sides accuse the other of breaking it.
By the end of the week, energy strikes are back, just maybe at a slightly lower peak than before.
Blunt read:
This is not a durable ceasefire. It is a pressure valve for global fuel markets and a diplomatic gift for Trump.

Likelihood:

High: some form of energy restraint is announced or implied.
Medium-high: it partially holds for a couple of days.
Medium: it collapses by the weekend or early next week.
Low: a solid, verified, long-term energy truce in just 7 days.
3) Russia will keep striking Ukraine hard, especially rail and western infrastructure
The train strike near Poland is very important. It shows Russia is willing to hit near NATO’s border, not just deep inside central Ukraine.

What I expect:

More Russian drone and missile attacks on Ukraine’s rail network, especially in the west and center.
Continued focus on energy, fuel stations, industrial hubs, and logistics.
More jet-powered drones, because they are harder to intercept and Russia is scaling production.
Strikes near Lviv, Poltava, Kyiv, and border regions will remain a priority.
Why:

Rail is Ukraine’s nervous system.
Western strikes hurt morale and complicate NATO support logistics.
A hit near Poland keeps NATO on edge without necessarily triggering Article 5.
Likelihood:

Very high: continued heavy Russian air campaign.
High: more rail/fuel/energy strikes.
Medium-high: another incident very close to Poland or another NATO border state.
Medium: a strike that causes significant political fallout in Europe.
4) Ukraine will keep hitting Russia deep, and Russia will feel it domestically
The headlines show Ukrainian drones reaching Arctic gas facilities, refineries, oil tankers, and other deep targets. That is not just symbolic. It is economic pressure.

What I expect next week:

More Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries, fuel depots, logistics hubs, and possibly northern/Arctic energy sites.
More air defense alerts in central and western Russia.
More regional complaints about blackout risk, fuel shortages, or price increases.
Kremlin spin will likely be “we expected this” and “the economy is fine,” but local governments will start taking more visible protective measures.
Domestic effect inside Russia:

Fuel prices may rise in some regions.
Authorities may introduce temporary export restrictions, price caps, or regional rationing if refinery damage is bad enough.
The ruble could come under pressure if sanctions talk intensifies.
People will notice more: longer lines at gas stations, higher transport costs, more complaints about drone noise and blackouts.
Likelihood:

High: continued Ukrainian deep strikes into Russia.
Medium-high: visible domestic fuel/economic stress in several regions.
Medium: government price controls or export limits on fuel products.
Low: a full-blown national fuel crisis in just one week, unless strikes hit multiple major refineries badly.
What will happen in Russia itself next week
5) More Russians will start leaving quietly, especially draft-age men and families
The AP/Ynetnews headlines about people fleeing abroad because of mobilization rumors are a big signal. This is not just “some guys with backpacks.” It is a social mood shift.

What I expect:

More young men, families with sons, and even some professionals will start looking at Georgia, Armenia, Kazakhstan, Turkey, Serbia, maybe the Gulf or Israel depending on passport options.
Border crossings and online chatter about “new mobilization” will increase after the election.
The Kremlin may either:
deny rumors hard, or
quietly prepare a partial call-up / reserve expansion without calling it “full mobilization.”
Blunt read:
Russia does not need to announce a new mobilization for people to start acting like one might come. Rumor is enough to move bodies.

Likelihood:

High: increased emigration interest and border movement.
Medium-high: more public talk about draft fears after the Duma vote.
Medium: small-scale reserve call-up or contract-soldier expansion announced within days or shortly after.
Low: full national mobilization declared in the next 7 days unless battlefield losses spike badly.
6) Internet and media controls will tighten a bit, especially around elections
The headlines mention shrinking freedoms and tighter internet controls. In election week, that usually means more censorship, more regional outages, and more pressure on social platforms.

What I expect:

More selective blocking or slowing of messengers/social apps in certain regions.
More state media messaging about stability, victory, and “the West wants to weaken Russia.”
More local officials telling people to vote.
More online suppression of anti-war content, especially if protests start.
Likelihood:

High: more censorship and election-week media pressure.
Medium: visible regional internet glitches or platform slowdowns.
Low: a major nationwide internet shutdown in just one week unless there is a serious protest wave.
War and NATO border dynamics
7) Poland will tighten the Ukraine border, and Russia will keep testing that edge
The drone hit on a passenger train near the Polish border is a classic “close but not quite” move. It creates fear without necessarily causing full escalation.

What I expect:

Poland reinforces security at the border with Ukraine.
More inspections, more checkpoints, maybe temporary restrictions on trucks or passenger crossings from western Ukraine.
NATO jets scramble more often near Lithuania, Poland, and the Baltic states.
Russia may allow another drone to enter or skim NATO airspace, then argue it was Ukrainian-made, civilian, or a technical failure.
Most likely result:

More tension, more political anger in Warsaw and Brussels.
No full border closure unless there is a bigger incident.
No direct NATO-Russia shootdown of a manned Russian aircraft in the next 7 days, because that would be a major escalation.
Likelihood:

High: Poland tightens border security.
Medium-high: another drone or airspace incident near Lithuania/Poland/Belarus.
Medium: temporary restrictions on crossings from Ukraine into Poland.
Low: full-scale NATO-Russia border clash in one week.
8) Russia will keep using the Baltic and Black Sea edges to signal strength
The Russian ship firing flares at a Danish helicopter, plus the drone over Lithuania, show Russia is willing to be annoying at the margins.

What I expect:

More Russian naval and electronic-warfare activity near the Baltics.
More “ambiguous” incidents that keep NATO on alert but stop short of open war.
Russia will use these moments to say the West is overreacting, while also showing it can project pressure close to Europe’s door.
Likelihood:

High: continued maritime/air irritations.
Medium: one incident becomes a big headline for 24–48 hours.
Low: a major naval confrontation in the next week.
Diplomacy and foreign policy
9) Putin-Zelensky meeting at G20 Miami is still unlikely next week
The Kremlin already says it is “impossible.” Zelensky wants it, but Moscow is not going to rush into a high-visibility summit this quickly.

What I expect:

No direct Putin-Zelensky meeting in the next 7 days.
More backchannel talks through US envoys: Witkoff, Kushner, and possibly CIA Director Ratcliffe taking a bigger role.
Maybe a phone call or small trilateral conversation, but not a dramatic summit.
Russia will use BRICS/India/China contacts to show it has other diplomatic lanes.
Blunt read:
Putin wants the option of talks, but he does not want to look like he is chasing Zelensky right after an election. Timing matters.

Likelihood:

High: no Putin-Zelensky meeting next week.
Medium: increased US-Russia-Ukraine backchannel activity.
Low: a sudden high-level bilateral summit in the next 7 days.
10) Russia will lean harder on India, China, and BRICS as a “parallel world” signal
The BRICS summit headlines show Putin getting a friendly reception in New Delhi, with India as a major trade lifeline and China still central to energy and components.

What I expect next week:

More Russian announcements about deals or cooperation with India and China.
Emphasis on oil trade, defense technology, drones, semiconductors/components, and local-currency settlement.
Russia will use this to say: “The West is not the only game in town.”
Likelihood:

High: more BRICS-related diplomacy and deal announcements.
Medium: new visible Russian-Indian or Russian-Chinese defense/tech cooperation.
Low: a sudden major de-dollarization breakthrough in just one week.
Sanctions, economy, and the US vote
11) The US House will likely take up the Russia sanctions bill next week
This is one of the clearest scheduled pressure points.

What I expect:

A House vote or serious move on the sanctions package late next week.
If it passes, Trump may sign it quickly to show strength, or he may tinker with it to preserve negotiating room.
The Treasury will likely add more designations around banks, shadow fleet vessels, oil logistics, and secondary-sanction targets.
Most likely result:

The bill becomes a real source of pressure on Russian energy and finance, even if implementation is messy.
Russia responds with de-dollarization rhetoric, local-currency pushes, and maybe new restrictions on fuel exports or payments.
Likelihood:

High: the House takes up the vote.
Medium-high: it passes or advances in a meaningful way.
Medium: Trump signs it within days of passage.
Low: it dies completely and becomes a non-event.
12) Russia’s domestic economy will feel more strain, especially around fuel
The combination of Ukrainian refinery strikes, global diesel price stress, and sanctions talk is a bad mix for Moscow.

What I expect next week:

More visible regional fuel-price pressure.
Government reassurances from oil companies and ministries.
Possible temporary measures:
export limits on diesel or other products,
price caps in some regions,
tighter controls on wholesale fuel markets.
The ruble may wobble a bit more if sanctions news gets stronger.
Blunt read:
Russia can absorb a lot, but fuel is politically sensitive. If people feel it at the pump, the government will start talking about “market stabilization” very quickly.

Likelihood:

High: continued economic pressure on Russia’s energy sector.
Medium-high: more visible domestic fuel/economic friction.
Medium: new official measures to calm fuel markets.
Low: a sudden full-scale currency or fuel crisis in just one week.
