Okay, straight answer, no fluff.

**Short prediction:**  
Next week in Iran will be a **diplomatic sprint plus managed pressure**. The regime will try to use the UN window in New York to get a face-saving deal around the Strait of Hormuz and oil shipping, while hardliners keep up small military squeezes so Tehran can say it is “not surrendering.” Inside Iran, expect **big state rallies, tighter security, a weaker rial, higher fuel/food prices, more border truck queues, and possibly a visible succession signal involving Mojtaba Khamenei**. No full collapse, no total ceasefire yet.

---

## Most likely things that will happen in Iran in the next week

### 1) The UN high-level session becomes the main diplomatic track
**Likelihood: high, maybe 65–75%.**

The fact that the US approved visas for top Iranian leaders to attend the UN General Assembly is a big signal. That means Washington is not trying to humiliate Tehran completely in public; it wants some kind of channel open.

Most likely:
- Masoud Pezeshkian and Abbas Araghchi, possibly other senior officials, will be in New York.
- There will be indirect talks with the US, Oman, Gulf states, maybe Qatar or Egypt behind the scenes.
- The main topic will not be “full peace” but a **temporary Hormuz shipping arrangement**.
- Iran will try to turn this into a public win: “We are at the table as equals.”
- The US will try to frame it as “Iran is moving toward the endgame.”

My read: by late in the week, there is a real chance of some kind of **technical agreement** or “framework” for escorted shipping through Hormuz. Not a full peace deal. More like:
- specific time windows for tankers,
- US naval protection at certain hours,
- Iran reducing fast-boat and drone harassment in exchange for some sanctions relief on oil, food imports, or currency access.

If Saudi Arabia comes back to the Oman/Gulf track, this becomes more likely. If Saudi stays out because of the pipeline attack and Houthi pressure, then the deal may be smaller: maybe just Iran-Oman-Qatar-US technical talks, with Gulf states watching nervously.

---

### 2) Huge state-organized rallies in Tehran and other cities
**Likelihood: very high, maybe 75–85%.**

The UN report on possible US war crimes is a gift to the regime right now. After the school strike in Minab and the Lamerd sports complex strike, Tehran can use this to say:
- “Look what they did.”
- “They are calling it war crimes, so we are not surrendering.”
- “The people stand with the nation.”

Expect:
- another massive rally in Tehran, probably again around major squares and government-organized bus convoys from the suburbs,
- rallies in Isfahan, Shiraz, Tabriz, Mashhad, Qom, Ahvaz,
- IRGC families, university students, bazaar merchants, clerical crowds, women in state-approved sections,
- slogans about “US war crimes,” “unity,” “not surrender,” and possibly “America out.”

The regime will use this to show that the country has not fractured. It wants the world to see: “Iran is still standing, and the people are with us.”

Security will be tight around embassies, universities, commercial centers, and places where opposition groups might try to organize.

---

### 3) Mojtaba Khamenei may become more visible
**Likelihood: medium, maybe 35–50%.**

The Financial Times piece about Mojtaba Khamenei “stirring unease” is important. The regime knows that after months of war, people are asking: what happens if the old guard gets hit? Who comes next?

Next week, there is a decent chance Tehran gives a **carefully staged succession signal**. Not an official announcement, but something like:
- Mojtaba appearing beside his father at a rally,
- a photo released from a military or clerical event,
- a short video showing him visiting IRGC units,
- or a public statement where Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei talks about “continuity” and “the next generation.”

This would be aimed at:
- hardliners, who want to know the system will survive,
- IRGC commanders,
- bazaar elites,
- families of war dead,
- and possibly the US/Israel, to show that succession is not chaotic.

If Mojtaba appears prominently, that is a big internal signal. It would mean the regime is preparing for a longer war or a leadership transition under controlled conditions.

---

### 4) Another naval skirmish off Qeshm or in Hormuz
**Likelihood: medium-high, maybe 55–65%.**

The headlines show this is already happening:
- explosions on Qeshm,
- an Iranian cargo ship struck,
- US forces destroying small Iranian boats trying to take a drone ship,
- Iran claiming it destroyed a US drone over Hormuz.

Next week, I would expect at least one more incident. Maybe:
- a fast boat chase,
- a drone shot down,
- a small merchant vessel damaged,
- a US escort action against Iranian boats,
- or another claimed “successful interception” by Iran.

But I do not think this becomes a full naval war in the next seven days unless there is a major tanker loss or a high-profile US/Israeli strike inside Iran.

The likely pattern is:
- both sides keep firing small shots,
- both claim victory,
- neither wants to make it so big that diplomacy dies.

Tehran will use these incidents to say: “We are still strong at sea.” Washington will say: “This is limited and controlled.”

### 5) The rial weakens further and economic pain becomes more visible
**Likelihood: very high, maybe 80–90%.**

This is probably the most important domestic story. War plus blockade plus oil above $100 means Iran is losing export revenue while import costs rise. Even if some oil moves through land routes, the system is strained.

Expect next week:
- further weakening of the rial in the open market,
- more complaints from bazaar merchants,
- higher prices for fuel, rice, cooking oil, medicine, construction materials,
- possible new government measures to control dollar sales,
- special import licenses for food and medicine,
- price-control announcements,
- maybe a temporary subsidy package.

The northern land routes through Turkey, Iraq, and possibly Azerbaijan will get more traffic, but they are not enough. The truck queues at Gürbulak and other crossings will become a visible domestic issue. If the Shalamcheh crossing with Iraq stays closed or reopens only partially, that will hurt Khuzestan trade and create local complaints.

The regime’s problem is simple: it wants to keep fuel cheap for the public, but the cost of importing substitutes is rising. That creates pressure on the bazaar. The bazaar is not the street movement, but if bazaar merchants get angry, the regime feels it quickly.

My guess: by midweek or late week, the central bank or government will announce some kind of economic relief package. It may be small, symbolic, and mostly about imports and subsidies.

### 6) Hardliners will push for cautious escalation before US midterms
**Likelihood: medium-high, maybe 50–60%.**

The Deutsche Welle headline is key: Iran appears to be boosting pressure on Washington ahead of the crucial midterm vote, but does not want full-scale war.

That means next week could see another round of proxy attacks:
- Houthi strikes toward Saudi Arabia or Red Sea shipping,
- Iraqi militia drone attacks on US-linked bases,
- maybe a missile or drone salvo at a Gulf base,
- possibly cyberattacks on energy or banking targets.

Tehran will probably deny direct responsibility for every attack, but it will not stop them either. The message is: “If you want to pressure us, we can make your allies hurt too.”

Inside Iran, this will show up as:
- IRGC statements,
- air-defense alerts in southern provinces,
- more missile/drone launch footage on state TV,
- claims of new underground missile assembly,
- propaganda about Russian Geran drones and Chinese satellite support.

This is not necessarily a move toward total war. It is calibrated pressure. Tehran wants to squeeze Trump before the midterms without making the US feel it must go all-in.

### 7) Nuclear diplomacy gets more tense after the IAEA snub
**Likelihood: medium, maybe 40–50%.**

Iran being blocked from the IAEA meeting at US urging is a diplomatic wound. Tehran will use it heavily.

Next week, expect:
- Iranian officials calling the IAEA process “political,”
- possibly a statement that Iran will “reassess its level of transparency,”
- maybe an announcement about increased enriched uranium stockpiles or new centrifuge installations,
- but probably not a full withdrawal from the NPT yet.

A full nuclear break would be a bigger escalation. I think Tehran is more likely to use the IAEA fight as leverage: “You blocked our chief, so do not act surprised if we slow down inspections.”

Unless the US or Israel strikes a nuclear site again in the next week, I would not expect Iran to fully abandon IAEA cooperation yet. But the tone will get harder.

### 8) Small opposition protests and counter-rallies, but likely contained
**Likelihood: medium, maybe 35–50%.**

The UN report also says Iranian authorities committed crimes against humanity during recent protest crackdowns. That gives opposition groups a strong narrative. Add the Jina Mahsa Amini anniversary mood, the school strike in Minab, inflation, and war fatigue, and there is potential for small counter-protests.

But I do not expect a full national street explosion next week unless one of these happens:
- a major price hike,
- a big funeral after a strike,
- a sudden rial crash,
- a visible leadership succession crisis,
- or a major internet shutdown during a protest.

More likely:
- small gatherings near universities in Tehran, Isfahan, Shiraz, or Tabriz,
- social media campaigns using the UN war crimes report,
- candles or silent vigils for protest victims and school children,
- “Woman, Life, Freedom” signs in semi-public spaces,
- quick arrests and short detentions,
- state TV showing unity rallies while trying to make opposition protests look small.

The regime will allow some visible opposition energy because it wants to show confidence: “We are not afraid.” But security will be ready to stop it from spreading.

### 9) Baha’i asset seizures and cultural crackdowns continue
**Likelihood: high, maybe 60–70%.**

The regime is using the war to tighten control over property, culture, and “foreign-linked” groups. The Baha’i asset seizure story fits that pattern.

Next week, expect:
- more court rulings or administrative orders affecting Baha’i properties,
- possible auctions or state takeover of assets,
- rights groups issuing statements,
- diaspora pressure campaigns,
- domestic media framing it as anti-corruption or “recovering stolen property.”

This may not trigger a huge street protest by itself, but it adds to the sense that the regime is squeezing critics while claiming unity. It also gives opposition figures another grievance to use alongside the UN report.

### 10) Diplomatic spats with Sweden and Europe continue
**Likelihood: high, maybe 70–80%.**

The expulsion of a Swedish diplomat and the Swedish move against an Iranian embassy employee show that even secondary diplomatic channels are getting tense.

Next week, expect:
- more Persian-language statements about “Western double standards,”
- possibly another Iranian diplomatic protest note,
- small demonstrations outside European embassies in Tehran,
- Iranian students or scholars in Europe becoming part of the propaganda fight.

This is not the main story, but it adds to the sense that Iran is fighting a war on many fronts: military, economic, nuclear, cultural, and diplomatic.

Backchannel talks with Oman, Gulf states, and the US will intensify. This is where the main branch happens.

**If talks make progress:**  
Iran may announce a narrow Hormuz shipping arrangement: escorted lanes, time windows, reduced harassment, some sanctions relief for oil or food imports. The regime will call it “not surrender,” but a “temporary regional understanding.” Oil prices may fall somewhat. Rallies continue, but the tone shifts to “we gained something at the table.”

**If talks stall:**  
Iran may launch a calibrated strike on a Gulf target, allow another Qeshm incident, or announce a nuclear/IAEA hardening step. Then Tehran will hold an even bigger rally and say the US is forcing Iran’s hand. The rial weakens more. Economic complaints rise.

The regime will try to consolidate domestically. Expect:
- more economic announcements,
- price-control measures,
- bazaar messaging,
- social media allowed to show unity but throttled during gatherings,
- reports of Baha’i asset seizures or new cultural crackdowns,
- possibly a visible appearance by Mojtaba Khamenei if the leadership wants to signal continuity.

## What could break this forecast

The biggest risk is a **major surprise strike** on Iran: Natanz, Isfahan, Tehran air defenses, southern ports, or even a targeted attack on a senior leader. If that happens in the next week, the whole picture changes. Then expect:
- full war footing domestically,
- rial crash,
- larger rallies,
- faster nuclear acceleration,
- more IRGC attacks on Gulf bases,
- stronger hardliner control over Pezeshkian’s government.

Another risk is a **major tanker loss** in Hormuz or Bab el-Mandeb. If a big oil shipment is hit and prices jump above $120, the pressure on Trump before midterms becomes intense. That could force either a bigger US strike or a faster deal.

A third risk is **Saudi Arabia staying out of the Hormuz talks** because of the pipeline attack and Houthi advance. If Saudi remains angry, the Gulf consensus Iran wants gets harder to build. Then the deal may be smaller or delayed.

A fourth risk is **internal succession anxiety**. If Mojtaba appears suddenly in a major public role, that could stabilize hardliners but alarm reformists and bazaar moderates. If he does not appear at all while war continues, some people will start asking harder questions about leadership continuity.

I would bet on this:

Inside Iran, that means:

- more truck queues at northern borders,
