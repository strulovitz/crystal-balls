Short version
India’s next week will likely be a three-front domestic pressure test:

Election Commission protests around CEC Gyanesh Kumar
Ujjain mosque demolition tensions
Women-safety anger after the Delhi park gang-rape allegation and university campus unrest
At the same time, the economy will show a strange split:

Strong industrial/IPO momentum

My main predictions for the next 7 days
1. The Election Commission crisis stays a top political story
This is the most important domestic political thread right now.

The opposition, Congress, and the “Cockroach Janta Party” / youth protest movement are pushing hard for Gyanesh Kumar to resign over voter-roll issues and alleged irregularities. Police have already used tear gas and detained protesters. That means this is no longer just a social-media fight; it has street politics attached to it.

What I expect next week
The CEC issue will remain one of the top 3 national stories in India for at least another 5–7 days.
The government will try to contain, delay, and dilute the protest momentum rather than immediately remove Gyanesh Kumar.
Opposition leaders and youth groups will likely announce new rallies, online campaigns, or a “final deadline” if he does not quit.
There will probably be more detentions in at least one or two cities, especially if protests hit Delhi, Kolkata, Mumbai, Bengaluru, or Lucknow.
The government may issue some kind of clarifying statement, set up an internal review, or say the process was “technical,” but I would not expect a sudden CEC replacement within 7 days unless the street pressure becomes really intense.
My confidence
85–90% that this remains a major headline next week
60–70% that protests continue in multiple cities
20–30% that Gyanesh Kumar resigns or is replaced within the next 7 days
The government’s instinct will probably be: outlast the protest, not lose face immediately.

2. Ujjain mosque demolition protests will keep simmering and may flare up again
The Ujjain story is dangerous because it combines:

Historic religious site
Partial demolition
Muslim volunteer self-demolition to prevent forced demolition
Police use of tear gas and batons
Hindu pilgrimage politics
Opposition attack on the BJP government
This is the kind of issue that can stay local for a while, but then suddenly become national if one image goes viral or someone gets seriously injured.

What I expect next week
More protests in Ujjain, probably daily or near-daily.
Police will likely try to keep the demolition process moving while managing crowds.
There is a real chance of another clash, especially if a larger crowd gathers on a religiously significant day.
The government may try to frame it as “restoration” or “heritage management” rather than “demolition.”
Opposition parties and social media will connect this with the Election Commission protests and women-safety anger to create a broader anti-government mood.
A court petition or stay application is possible, but I would not expect a clean legal resolution within 7 days unless things get very heated.
My confidence
80% that Ujjain remains a major story next week
50–60% that there is at least one notable protest clash or police action
30–40% that it becomes a larger national communal flashpoint within the week
If someone dies, or if a very symbolic part of the mosque is damaged in a way that looks aggressive online, this can jump quickly.

Allegation
Campus or local protest
Police delay or perceived slow action
Social media explosion
Opposition demands for fast-track courts / committees / safety task force

What I expect next week
More protests in Delhi, probably outside a police station, court, or university campus.
Police will likely file FIRs and may arrest some accused to show action.
The university may extend suspension of classes or announce a security/complaint redressal review.
Women’s groups, opposition parties, and online activists will push for stronger safety measures, not just punishment.
The government will probably announce some kind of task force, special investigation, or campus safety guideline.
If the victim family goes public, or if accused try to flee, the protests can intensify quickly.
My confidence
75% that women-safety protests remain visible next week
60–70% that police make at least some arrests or file formal charges by midweek
40–50% that this becomes a broader national debate on campus safety and public park policing
This issue will not disappear just because other stories appear. It has emotional momentum.

The Bloomberg headline about forex reserves falling as the RBI supports the rupee amid surging oil prices is a big warning sign. Combine that with:

Russian oil supply tightening
Strong Chinese demand for Russian crude
Surging power demand
Heat stress
Imported coal blending debate
Captive coal plants ordered to run at maximum capacity
and you get an economy that is growing well but facing external pressure.

What I expect next week
The rupee will likely remain under pressure, especially if oil prices stay elevated or rise further.
The RBI may continue intervening in the forex market.
Forex reserves may fall again if crude stays high and capital outflows persist.
Power demand will remain strong into early October, especially in northern, central, and western India if heat persists.
The government may move faster on imported coal blending or emergency coal dispatch to keep power plants running.
Some industrial states may see complaints about power reliability, even if the official narrative remains “demand is being managed.”
My confidence
80% that rupee stress continues next week
70% that RBI intervention continues
65–70% that the government makes some public move on coal blending, imported coal use, or emergency power measures
This is not a crisis yet, but it is definitely a strain.

The Essar/Iowa/Russia story will be used by media and opposition to question India’s industrial diplomacy.
Domestic steel companies may lobby for policy clarity on capacity expansion, imports, anti-dumping rules, and green steel.

My confidence

30–40% that the Essar-Russia-US angle gets more political attention
