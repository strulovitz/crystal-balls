One-line forecast
Israel will likely spend the next seven days in controlled escalation: it will hit back at Britain/Europe over settlement sanctions, keep striking Hamas/Hezbollah/Iranian-proxy targets in Gaza, Lebanon and possibly Syria/Iraq, manage rising Iran–Houthi–Red Sea risk, and turn the UAE-warning story into a brutal pre-election political fight.

Scenario weights
60%: controlled escalation — more diplomatic friction, continued selective strikes, domestic political firestorm, no decisive breakthrough.

Most likely things that will happen in Israel next week
1. The UK/Europe settlement-sanctions row will escalate symbolically
Confidence: very high — ~85–90%

This is the biggest diplomatic story. Israel will not just shrug off the UK, France, Canada and others banning settlement goods/services. Expect:

Formal Israeli diplomatic protests and sharper public language from Foreign Minister Gideon Saar and Prime Minister Netanyahu.
More concrete Israeli countermeasures against Britain, possibly expanded bans on British MPs/diplomats entering Israel.
Preparations for the 30-day closure deadline for the British East Jerusalem consulate. The actual closure likely lands closer to early October, but next week will be full of legal, administrative and symbolic wrangling.
Trade friction over “settlement origin” goods: wine, agriculture, services, tech-linked contracts, customs disputes.
More third-country port seizures or delays, like the Malaysia container story. Expect shipping/customs headaches to become a recurring headline.
Israel pushing harder for diplomatic wins elsewhere — Japan, Greece, Slovenia, Pacific states, smaller EU countries — to offset the Western European blow.
Blunt read: Israel will treat this as both an economic nuisance and a political insult. It will retaliate where it can hurt symbolically without opening a full trade war.

2. Gaza operations will continue despite “ceasefire” language
Confidence: high — ~80–85%

The headlines already show strikes continuing even under a US-brokered ceasefire arrangement. That means the word “ceasefire” is doing a lot of work. Next week, expect:

Continued Israeli airstrikes on Hamas and possibly Islamic Jihad targets.
At least one significant strike that gets international attention — either because of the target’s rank, the location, or civilian casualties.
More pressure around crossings, especially with Yom Kippur likely falling in this window. Kerem Shalom and other crossing disruptions will feed humanitarian complaints from NGOs.
Behind-the-scenes hostage diplomacy may continue, but I would not bet on a major breakthrough unless something leaks or a senior figure is killed.
What I think is most likely:
Israel will keep saying it is acting against “military targets,” while the public and opposition argue that every strike deepens the political crisis before the October election.

3. Lebanon: no Rome deal next week, more strikes and UNIFIL friction
Confidence: high — ~75–80%

The Israel–Lebanon talks in Rome are postponed, so next week is likely to be a mix of:

Continued Israeli airstrikes in southern Lebanon.
More tunnel/fortification demolition work, possibly with small seismic effects and local complaints.
Lebanese Army claims about weapons seizures and “operational control” in pilot zones.
France and Lebanon pushing to extend or preserve UNIFIL’s role despite Israeli opposition.
Informal Security Council diplomacy may begin, but a formal mandate decision is probably still later.
Blunt read: Israel wants leverage before any real agreement. It will keep hitting Hezbollah-linked targets while trying to make Beirut feel the cost of delay.

4. Syria and Jordan: more friction, not necessarily war
Confidence: medium-high — ~65–75%

Netanyahu’s visit to troops in southern Syria, especially Mount Hermon, will keep Syrian anger alive. Expect:

Syrian diplomatic protests over what it calls an “illegal” entry.
More Israeli operations in the buffer zone / southern Syria to destroy tunnels or fortifications.
Possible minor clashes or artillery/fire incidents along the Golan/southern Syria area.
Jordan becoming a more sensitive issue because of the water-deal lapse, border tensions, and the reported Iranian strike on Jordan that damaged US military aircraft.
A full Israel–Syria war is not my base case, but I would not be surprised by another low-level incident that becomes a big headline.

5. Iran, Houthis, Red Sea and Hormuz risk will stay very high
Confidence: high for volatility — ~70–80%

This is the biggest “surprise escalation” cluster. Several things point to continued pressure:

Trump saying the Iran war will end “immediately” after US midterms suggests the US wants pressure but not full-scale escalation before November.
IAEA observing construction at Pickaxe Mountain will feed Israeli claims that Iran is rebuilding nuclear/missile capability.
Pezeshkian saying Iran will not surrender means Tehran is trying to project resilience, not collapse.
Houthi seizure of Mokha and alerts near Saudi Arabia mean the Red Sea/Bab el-Mandab risk is rising.
Gulf states and Iran may meet next week on a temporary Hormuz shipping arrangement.
Most likely next-week outcome:
A mix of diplomacy, oil-price jitters, shipping disruption, and possible limited strikes on Houthi or Iranian-proxy targets in Iraq/Syria/Yemen.

Less likely but real risk:
A direct Israel–Iran exchange — missiles, drones, underground-site strikes, or a larger retaliatory salvo. I’d put that around 25–35% for some noticeable exchange in the next seven days, not a full war.

6. Domestic politics will get ugly fast: Oct 7, UAE warning, election, Naza film
Confidence: very high — ~80–90%

This is where Israel’s internal story becomes as important as the military one.

Netanyahu / UAE warning
The report that the UAE warned Netanyahu before October 7 is a political grenade. Expect:

Opposition leaders doubling down on “Netanyahu unfit” rhetoric.
More media fights over what was said, when, and who knew what.
A formal defamation case against Haaretz likely announced or filed soon.
Netanyahu using the fight to reframe the narrative: either “I was warned and acted” or “they are attacking me because I am winning.”
Election politics
With elections coming up on October 27, the next week will be full of:

Campaign speeches.
Front-line visits to project strength.
Coalition math talk around Raam, Bennett’s Together party, Golan/Democrats, and other fragments.
The opposition trying to make October 7 the central issue.
Netanyahu trying to keep the focus on security, economy, and “strength under pressure.”
Naza documentary controversy
The Gaza documentary “Naza” is already causing a cultural-political storm. Expect:

More attacks from Israeli ministers calling the filmmakers traitors or seeking to strip citizenship.
Legal petitions or public protests.
The film becoming a proxy battle over memory, censorship, and wartime decision-making.
International media using it as evidence of internal Israeli division.
Blunt read: The government will try to turn outrage into election fuel. The opposition will try to turn every strike and every leaked warning into proof that the leadership is weak or out of touch.

More IDF arrests, raids, and checkpoint confrontations.
More settler violence incidents, possibly with video going viral.

E1 / Khan al-Ahmar remaining a flashpoint because of demolition orders and international attention.
Possible Israeli settlement announcements or fast-tracked planning to show it is not backing down.
A massive new land-seizure announcement is possible, but I would not put it as the most likely single event unless Israel wants to make a very loud pre-election statement.

The most likely next week for Israel is: a tense, multi-front week with diplomatic friction, selective strikes, oil/Red Sea jitters, and a vicious pre-election political fight — but no single decisive endgame unless Iran, Hamas, or Hezbollah forces one.
