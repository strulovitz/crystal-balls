Most likely things that happen next week
1. More public statements that the US-Vietnam trade deal is close
This is the highest-probability item.

To Lam has already said negotiations are positive and “very close.” Expect more of that in the next few days, probably in one of these forms:

A new statement from To Lam or a senior official.
A follow-up comment after his New York/Ottawa trip.
State media framing the US relationship as “breakthrough,” “progress,” or “near completion.”
More talk about Vietnam buying more US goods, especially aircraft, technology, and energy products, to narrow the trade imbalance.

If a formal framework or near-final text appears, that would be a big positive shock for Vietnamese assets and sentiment.

2. The stock market stays volatile, with continued post-upgrade adjustment
The FTSE upgrade is a milestone, but the market has already shown the classic problem: after a long-awaited event, some investors sell into strength.

Headlines say Vietnamese stocks fell 2.2% in the first week after the upgrade. That suggests the next week could still be choppy.

Likely scenarios:

The VN-Index probably stays volatile, maybe moving within a rough -3% to +2% range over several sessions.
Foreign flows may remain mixed: some passive buying from index funds, but also profit-taking and retail churn.
Bank stocks, securities firms, real estate names, and export-oriented companies could see elevated trading.
If the market falls too sharply, you may hear more official reassurance or talk about improving market rules.
The headline that Vietnam is “weighing a wider daily trading band” is important. That suggests regulators are thinking about making the market look more modern and attractive to foreign investors.

My prediction: in the next week, we may not get a full policy announcement, but we could see:

More official comments about market reforms.
A consultation or pilot discussion on wider price limits.
State media praising liquidity and investor interest even if the index is soft.
In blunt terms: the upgrade helps Vietnam’s long-term credibility, but next week may still be messy.

3. Government bond market activity continues
The $1 billion government bond auction being the highest volume this year is a meaningful signal. It suggests:

The state is actively managing fiscal financing.
There is enough domestic appetite for Vietnamese debt.
International credibility is improving around Vietnam’s macro story.
Next week, I would expect:

More discussion of sovereign debt issuance.
Possible follow-up auctions or investor roadshows.
Foreign institutional interest in Vietnamese government bonds if the FTSE upgrade improves overall country perception.
This is a quieter but important part of the story. Vietnam is not just trying to attract equity investors; it is also trying to look like a more credible emerging-market debt market.

4. Typhoon aftermath becomes a major domestic news theme
The typhoon headline about coastal destruction suggests that disaster management will be front and center, especially in central Vietnam.

Over the next week, likely developments include:

Official damage assessments from affected provinces.
State media reports on housing, roads, power lines, irrigation, agriculture, and fisheries losses.
Relief packages, emergency funds, or material support announced by central authorities.
A senior leader or prime minister inspecting affected areas.
Possible follow-up rain warnings if the weather pattern stays active.
This matters because typhoons affect:

Food prices.
Logistics.
Energy supply.
Infrastructure costs.
Investor perception of climate risk.
If the damage is severe, you may see more talk about rebuilding with better standards, “green” reconstruction, or climate-resilient infrastructure. That would fit Vietnam’s broader push to attract foreign capital while managing physical risks.

5. North Korea’s defense visit produces a low-key but meaningful diplomatic signal
The news that North Korea’s vice defence minister is visiting Vietnam is not huge, but it is notable in the current regional security environment.

My prediction:

The visit will likely be brief and formal.
There may be meetings with Vietnamese military or top political leaders.
Official statements will probably emphasize “cooperation,” “regional peace,” and “security exchange.”
Vietnam will avoid sounding too aligned with North Korea, especially while it is courting the US, Japan, Canada, and Europe.
In other words: expect a diplomatic photo-op, not a major arms deal announcement.

The subtext is that Vietnam is trying to maintain balance among great powers. It wants US trade progress, Japanese banking investment, Canadian strategic partnership, European trade interest, and still keeps channels open with North Korea and Russia.

Possible next-week outcomes:

A public confirmation that talks are progressing.
A term sheet or framework announcement.
More analyst commentary and bank stock volatility.
Regulatory approval discussions if the deal moves forward.
My guess: a full final deal may not be signed in just 7 days, but there is a decent chance of another visible update. If that happens, VPBank could become one of the most-watched stocks in Vietnam next week.

This also feeds into the broader narrative: Vietnam’s private banking sector is still attractive to foreign strategic investors, especially Japanese and Western banks looking for Asia exposure.

7. Tesla / VinFast rivalry gets more attention

Next week, likely developments:

More media discussion about Tesla entering the market.
VinFast responding with marketing, pricing, or product news.
Government or business community talk about charging infrastructure, local supply chains, and import rules.
Possible state media framing Vietnam as an EV hub in Southeast Asia.

That narrative is useful for Hanoi because it shows competition, modernization, and foreign confidence.

Next week, possible signals:

More official statements about long-term energy planning.
Discussion of nuclear feasibility, investment structures, or international partners.
Energy deals framed as part of the US-Vietnam relationship, especially after the ExxonMobil crude oil agreement.
A full nuclear announcement in 7 days is less likely, but more talk is very possible.

The headlines about To Lam upgrading Canada to a strategic partner and the EU advancing free trade with Southeast Asia suggest Vietnam is not just chasing one big relationship. It is diversifying.

Next week, expect:

More state media coverage of Vietnam’s broadened diplomatic outreach.
Talks about investment in technology, energy, agriculture, critical minerals, or finance.
Possibly more business delegations or MOUs connected to the Ottawa visit.
Continued emphasis on “strategic autonomy” — Vietnam wants to be courted by many powers at once.
