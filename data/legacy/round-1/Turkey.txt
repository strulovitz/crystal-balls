Okay, bluntly and without the politically correct wrapper: assuming “next week” means roughly the next 7 days from 1 October 2026, Turkey will mostly be dominated by the investment-fund / stock-market scandal, not football, not the comedian case, and not even the Saudi-Pakistan-Turkey military pact. The fund crisis is now the main political-economy story, and Erdogan’s government will try to turn it into a “manipulators and rogue financiers” story while quietly buying time with payments, asset freezes/unfreezes, and media management.

My base-case prediction for the next week:

Turkey will begin or formalize first interim payments to trapped investment-fund investors
Confidence: very high, around 85-90%
The Bloomberg headline saying Turkey is starting interim payments of up to 1 million lira is the most important one in your list. Next week you should expect the government and markets regulator to explain how those payments work: which banks will process them, which funds qualify, what documents investors need, whether payments are proportional, and whether more tranches will follow.

What this will probably produce:

bank app queues and complaints
confusion over who gets paid first
small investors feeling angry if the payment is only partial
larger investors and fund managers trying to negotiate better treatment
social media fights about whether this is “compensation” or a “bailout”
Erdogan will likely frame it as: the economy is strong, the board is working, and the people who manipulated the market will be punished. That is the political line he needs.

More asset freezes, unfreezes, legal battles, and name-dropping in the fund scandal
Confidence: high, around 75-80%
The fact that asset freezes were lifted on 46 companies while a former minister’s assets were frozen shows this is not a clean judicial process. It is becoming a political-economic negotiation. Some companies will be unfrozen to limit market damage. Others will stay frozen as pressure tools. More names from fund managers, brokerage houses, bankers, and possibly party-connected figures may appear.

Next week likely includes:

more court decisions on asset freezes
companies suing or appealing freeze orders
prosecutors naming more people in the Tera Yatirim / fund crisis circle
media leaks about who is “safe” and who is being squeezed
AKP insiders trying to distance themselves from the worst names
The political risk for Erdogan is that the scandal stops looking like a small group of rogue financiers and starts looking like a wider network around the government. The fact that a deputy chair of AK Party resigned and then an ex-minister’s assets were frozen shows the pressure is reaching the ruling party.

AK Party will manage internal fallout quietly, but expect more reshuffling or quiet resignations
Confidence: medium-high, around 60-70%
The resignation of Fatma Betul Sayan Kaya after trading allegations is a sign that the government is trying to show accountability without opening a full civil war inside AKP. Next week I would expect either:

another quiet resignation or role change
a reshuffling of economic officials
a new technical committee or “board” being given more public visibility
Erdogan meeting separately with economic managers, party bosses, and market figures
I do not expect a full cabinet collapse next week. That would be too big unless the fund crisis suddenly hits banks or retail investors much harder than expected. But I do expect AKP to keep rotating people around the scandal so the government can say: “We are cleaning house.”

Social media and media friction will increase, especially around X and T24
Confidence: high, around 80%
The X blocking of nearly 500 accounts from journalists, economists, academics, and public figures is a big signal. The government is trying to control the narrative during a financial crisis. Next week you should expect:

more X account blocks or temporary suspensions
complaints from economists and journalists
possibly some accounts being restored after backlash
government hints that social media platforms need to be “responsible”
T24’s block becoming a symbol of media pressure
The T24 case is interesting because it was blocked over alleged LGBTQ+ propaganda. That gives the government a cultural-warfare cover story while also looking like censorship to urban, secular, and business audiences. Next week T24 may appeal, use alternative domains, or publish content through other platforms. Other outlets may test the limits too.

The comedian case will stay politically useful but not explode into a new street movement by itself
Confidence: medium-high, around 70%
Deniz Göktaş being convicted but released pending appeal is a very Turkish judicial outcome. It lets the government say justice was done while avoiding keeping him in prison and turning him into a full martyr. Next week likely includes:

an appeal hearing or procedural decision
him staying free for now, probably with travel restrictions
opposition parties using the case to attack Erdogan’s justice system
AKP base saying he insulted the president and religious values, so he should have been punished harder
This case will remain a cultural symbol, but I would not expect it alone to trigger massive protests next week unless the fund crisis makes people very angry at the same time.

Markets will stay nervous; small caps will suffer more than large caps
Confidence: high, around 75-80%
The fund crisis is already hitting smaller, less liquid stocks harder. That is a classic sign of stress in the market structure. Next week I expect:

BIST volatility
weakness in small and mid caps
possible selective support for large banks or blue chips
lira pressure if investors worry about fiscal costs
central bank verbal support or liquidity management
The key question is whether the interim payments are seen as a temporary pain relief or as the start of a bigger fiscal burden. If markets believe the government has a credible plan, the lira may stabilize. If they think more losses will be hidden in banks, insurers, or state-linked funds, pressure will return.

Turkey will posture strongly on the Saudi-Pakistan-Houthi file but avoid direct war next week
Confidence: medium-high, around 65-70%
The Turkey-Saudi Arabia-Pakistan pact is being tested by Houthi attacks. Ankara wants to show the pact is real and useful, but it does not want to be dragged into a Yemen war or an Iran conflict without very clear gains. Next week likely includes:

urgent talks in Istanbul or Riyadh
intelligence-sharing arrangements
possible Turkish military assets offered to Saudi Arabia, such as drones, air defense systems, or naval/logistics support
joint statements saying the pact is active and serious
Pakistan staying loud but cautious
I do not expect Turkey to formally commit ground troops to fight Houthis next week unless there is a major escalation. More likely: Ankara becomes a coordinator, mediator, and supplier of military capability while keeping its options open.

The US blockade on Iran will make Turkey more important as a trade corridor, but also create border pain
Confidence: high, around 75%
The truck queues at the Turkey-Iran border are a real sign that the US naval pressure on Iranian ports is pushing trade overland through Turkey and Pakistan. Next week likely includes:

continued truck jams at eastern border crossings
complaints from logistics companies and truck drivers
debate over customs fees, transit charges, and border procedures
Ankara using its geographic position as leverage in Iran-related diplomacy
more talk about alternative routes through the Caspian Sea or Central Asia
This is not just an Iran story. It affects Turkey’s trade, import costs, logistics sector, and regional bargaining power. If the queues get bad enough, you may see trucker protests or temporary border measures.

Football refereeing arrests will become a secondary scandal, but probably not the main one
Confidence: medium, around 50-60%
The arrest of the head of Turkey’s soccer refereeing body and his deputy is significant, but it is unlikely to dominate the national news above the fund crisis. Still, next week may bring:

more arrests or statements from the investigation
the football federation creating an interim committee or special commission
debates about referee appointments and VAR decisions
possible attempts by opposition parties to turn it into a corruption story
If the probe expands quickly, it could become another layer of “the system is being cleaned up” or “the system is attacking institutions,” depending on who you ask.

Erdogan will keep using foreign policy to show strength while the domestic economy is under stress
Confidence: high, around 80%
Erdogan needs external wins because the fund crisis is creating internal pressure. Next week he will likely emphasize:

Turkey’s role in the Muslim world
the Saudi-Pakistan-Turkey pact
mediation between Iran and regional actors
Turkey’s importance in trade routes under the US-Iran blockade
diplomatic meetings with European or German figures
The Germany headline about Erdogan meeting a potential next German leader is also useful for him. It lets him present Turkey as a major regional player that Europe must take seriously. Next week may produce more statements about EU-Turkey relations, but I would not expect a breakthrough deal in one week.

What will probably NOT happen next week:

A full resolution of the fund crisis. The payment plan will be partial and technical.
A sudden AKP government collapse unless bank exposure or retail panic gets much worse.
A massive nationwide protest wave solely because of the comedian case.
Turkey directly entering a war against Houthis or Iran in the next 7 days.
A complete unblocking of T24 or X accounts unless the backlash becomes very loud.
A major constitutional or electoral change.
But there are three big shock scenarios to watch:

Shock scenario 1: Bank exposure is revealed
If people start discovering that banks, insurers, or state-linked funds have large hidden losses from the failed investment funds, the story can move from “rich investors trapped” to “ordinary savers at risk.” That would raise the chance of bigger protests and faster government action.

Shock scenario 2: The lira falls sharply
If the interim payments are seen as a fiscal burden, or if foreign investors lose confidence, the lira could weaken quickly. Then the central bank may have to intervene more visibly, possibly through reserves or short-term rates. That would make the next week much more intense.

Shock scenario 3: Houthi attacks escalate badly
If Houthis hit Saudi oil infrastructure hard enough, Turkey and Pakistan may be forced into a stronger military role. Ankara might then announce drone deployments, air defense support, or joint operations. That would change Turkey’s foreign policy posture quickly.
